Why documentary

A year from now you won't remember the seating chart. You'll remember your mother fixing a strap that didn't need fixing. Your best friend mouthing oh my god from the third row. The exact second the band played the song. These photographs are for that.

The day

One wedding, told the way it unfolded.

  1. 1:40pm

    Getting ready

    The room is too small and full of people talking at once. Someone's steaming a dress. Someone's crying already. I stay out of the way and keep watching.

  2. 4:02pm

    The aisle

    Forty seconds, give or take. Nobody gets to do it again. I'll be where I need to be so you don't have to think about me at all.

  3. 4:31pm

    Right after

    The recessional ends and there's a few seconds where it's just the two of you, a bit stunned. I've learned to be ready for it.

  4. 5:15pm

    Portraits, briefly

    This is the one part I direct. It still shouldn't feel like posing: we walk, we talk, I give you something to do. Twenty minutes and you're back with your guests.

  5. 7:48pm

    The toasts

    Somebody always cries, and it's never who you'd guess. I spend the speeches watching the room, not the microphone.

  6. 10:26pm

    The party

    Nobody's thinking about photos anymore, which is exactly when they get good. The light is bad and the room is loud. That's fine. I bring flash and I'm not afraid of the dark.

  7. 11:52pm

    Last song

    The room empties out. Shoes off, jackets on the wrong people. What's left is how it felt, and that's what I'm after.

How I work

I shoot the whole minute. You get the second that mattered.

A moment isn't one frame, it's a dozen. The half-blink, the laugh starting, the laugh, the laugh falling apart. I go through every one and mark the keepers by hand, the way it used to be done on a contact sheet.

Should we do a first look?

It gives you a calmer timeline and lets you actually go to your own cocktail hour. Skipping it keeps the reveal at the aisle. Both are good choices, and we'll figure out which suits you on our call.

Our venue is really dark. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Candlelit barns, tents, basements with one string of lights: I bring my own light and know how to use the dark. Some couples book me for exactly that look.

Do we need a planner?

Get at least a day-of coordinator. Without one, the vendors end up running the wedding on the fly. I'll work directly with whoever you hire.

What about guests on their phones?

An unplugged ceremony keeps the aisle clear and the faces visible. For the reception, phones are fine. A friend with a film camera on the dance floor is always welcome.

Do you travel?

Yes. Anywhere in Gauteng is included, from Joburg and Pretoria to the Cradle and the Magalies. Further afield, like the Winelands, the Midlands or the Bushveld, I add a flat travel fee that you'll see up front.
